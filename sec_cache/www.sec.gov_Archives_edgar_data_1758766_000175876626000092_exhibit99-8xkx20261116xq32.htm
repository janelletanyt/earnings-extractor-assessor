Stem Announces Third Quarter 2026 Results
PowerTrackTM software revenue up 18% YoY
Achieved 40 gigawatts of solar assets under management
Sharpening focus on PowerTrack by de-emphasizing Managed Services and battery hardware resale
Raising full-year non-GAAP gross margin and adjusted EBITDA guidance; lowering revenue, ARR, and operating cash flow guidance

HOUSTON – November 16, 2026 – Stem, Inc. (“Stem,” “we” or the “Company”) (NYSE: STEM), a global leader in AI-enabled clean energy software and services, today announced its results for the quarter ended September 30, 2026.
Financial Highlights
•Revenue of $36.7 million, down 4% from $38.2 million in 3Q25
•Software, services, and edge hardware revenue of $35.3 million, up 4% from $34.0 million in 3Q25
•GAAP gross profit of $16.8 million, up 24% from $13.5 million in 3Q25
•GAAP gross margin of 46%, up from 35% in 3Q25
•Non-GAAP gross profit of $21.2 million, up 18% from $17.9 million in 3Q25
•Non-GAAP gross margin of 58%, up from 47% in 3Q25
•Net loss of $14.6 million versus net loss of $23.8 million in 3Q25
•Adjusted EBITDA of $7.1 million, up from $2.0 million in 3Q25
•Positive operating cash flow of $1.5 million versus $11.4 million in 3Q25
•Ended 3Q26 with $36.9 million in cash and cash equivalents versus $38.4 million in 2Q26

Operating Highlights
•Bookings of $27.6 million, down from $36.8 million in 2Q26
•Contracted backlog of $22.3 million, down from $27.1 million at the end of 2Q26
•Contracted annual recurring revenue (“CARR”) of $70.5 million, up from $69.0 million at the end of 2Q26
•Annual recurring revenue (“ARR”) of $64.0 million, up 3% from $62.4 million at the end of 2Q26
•Solar operating AUM of 40.0 gigawatts (“GW”), up 4% sequentially
•Storage operating assets under management (“AUM”) of 1.8 gigawatt hours (“GWh”), flat sequentially


“Our third quarter shows the operating leverage of a business built around PowerTrack. PowerTrack software revenue grew 18% year-over-year, we achieved a record 40 gigawatts of solar assets under management, and we were selected for the first utility-scale solar project in the Republic of Georgia,” stated Arun Narayanan, Chief Executive Officer of Stem. “We are sharpening our focus on PowerTrack, our highest-margin and highest-growth offering, and de-emphasizing Managed Services and battery hardware resale. PowerTrack remains the leading solution for C&I solar monitoring in the United States with 70% market share, and we are driving expansion across utility-scale and hybrid deployments in the US and internationally. As announced on October 26, our Board has initiated a review of strategic alternatives to maximize shareholder value. Throughout that process, our team remains focused on execution and on serving our customers.”

“We delivered another quarter of strong margins and adjusted EBITDA, with non-GAAP gross margin of 58% and adjusted EBITDA of $7.1 million, more than triple the third quarter of 2025,” stated Brian Musfeldt, Chief Financial Officer of Stem. “Year-to-date adjusted EBITDA of $15.3 million has already exceeded the top of our original full-year range, and we generated positive operating cash flow of $1.5 million in the quarter. We are raising our non-GAAP gross margin and adjusted EBITDA guidance to reflect a revenue mix weighted toward higher-margin PowerTrack solutions. We are lowering revenue, ARR and operating cash flow guidance to reflect the de-emphasis of battery hardware resale and Managed Services, including the exit of the majority of our remaining legacy energy storage financing entities. These changes are expected to leave us with a more focused, higher-margin business.”

Third Quarter 2026 Financial and Operating Results
Financial Results
Revenue decreased 4% year-over-year to $36.7 million, versus $38.2 million in the third quarter of 2025, driven by significantly reduced battery hardware resales. Revenue from software, services, and edge hardware was $35.3 million for the third quarter of 2026, up 4% from $34.0 million for the third quarter of 2025, driven by 18% year-over-year growth in PowerTrack software revenue.
GAAP gross profit was $16.8 million, or 46%, versus $13.5 million, or 35%, in the third quarter of 2025. The year-over-year increase in GAAP gross profit ($) and GAAP gross margin (%) was driven by reduced costs, increased higher-margin software, services, and edge hardware sales, and reduced lower-margin battery hardware resale revenue.
Non-GAAP gross profit was $21.2 million, or 58%, versus $17.9 million, or 47%, in the third quarter of 2025. The year-over-year increase in non-GAAP gross profit ($) was driven by increased higher-margin software, services, and edge hardware sales. The year-over-year increase in non-GAAP gross margin (%) reflects increased higher-margin software, services, and edge hardware sales, along with reduced lower-margin battery hardware resale revenue.
Net loss was $14.6 million versus the third quarter of 2025 net loss of $23.8 million. The year-over-year improvement was primarily driven by increased GAAP gross profit, lower operating expenses, the gain on sale of energy storage systems related to the exit of our legacy energy storage financing entities, and an impairment of energy storage systems related to the de-emphasis of Managed Services in the third quarter of 2026.
Adjusted EBITDA was $7.1 million, up from $2.0 million in the third quarter of 2025. The year-over-year improvement was primarily driven by a higher mix of high-margin software and services revenue combined with lower operating expenses.

The Company ended the third quarter with $36.9 million in cash and cash equivalents, versus $38.4 million reported at the end of the second quarter of 2026.
Operating Results
Bookings were $27.6 million in the third quarter of 2026, down from $36.8 million in the second quarter of 2026. The sequential decrease was driven by reduced Managed Services and battery hardware resale bookings due to the de-emphasis of these offerings, and softness in US solar bookings as a result of the investment tax credit (“ITC”) phase out.
Contracted backlog was $22.3 million at the end of the third quarter of 2026, down from $27.1 million at the end of the second quarter of 2026. The sequential decrease was driven by the removal of battery hardware resale bookings, reduced domestic solar bookings as a result of the ITC phase out, and quarterly project commissioning.
CARR was $70.5 million at the end of the third quarter of 2026, up 2% from $69.0 million at the end of the second quarter of 2026.
ARR increased 3% to $64.0 million at the end of the third quarter of 2026 from $62.4 million at the end of the second quarter of 2026. PowerTrack ARR increased 4% sequentially to $44.4 million at the end of the third quarter of 2026 from $42.8 million at the end of the second quarter of 2026. Managed services ARR was sequentially flat at $19.6 million.
Solar operating AUM increased 4% sequentially to 40.0 GW for the third quarter of 2026. Storage operating AUM was sequentially flat at 1.8 GWh.

Business Updates

•Stem is further concentrating its investment and attention on PowerTrack, its highest-margin and highest-growth offering, and is de-emphasizing its Managed Services and battery hardware resale offerings.

◦The Company is no longer pursuing new Managed Services contracts or battery hardware resales. The Company will continue to support its existing Managed Services customers.

◦On November 16, 2026, the Company announced the sale of the intellectual property associated with Athena, its distributed battery optimization platform, to Voltus, Inc., a leading distributed energy resource solution provider. The transaction was entirely a cash consideration valued at $10 million. This transaction monetizes technology that sits outside the core PowerTrack platform, strengthens the Company’s balance sheet, and supports continued investment in PowerTrack.

3

◦The Company has disposed of the economic interests in the majority of its consolidated Special Purpose Entities, or SPE’s. These are legacy energy storage system financing entities which Stem set up with outside investors between 2015 and 2017 to fund and monetize energy storage systems. Exiting the Company’s interest in these entities, removes approximately $30 million in liabilities, has no negative impact on cash billings, simplifies the financial statements, and is a key step in de-emphasizing the Managed Services business. The Company expects a reduction of approximately $3 million in fourth quarter managed services revenue and approximately $10 million in year-end annual recurring revenue as a result of the disposal of these interests.

•PowerTrack remains the leading solution for C&I solar monitoring in the United States with solutions that connect field assets, cloud data, system controls, and operational workflows in one platform interface across solar, storage and hybrid portfolios. Stem serves both C&I and utility-scale customers across 55 countries.

◦On November 16, the Company announced it had entered into an agreement with Copec Flux, the renewable energy subsidiary of Copec S.A., to jointly deliver energy storage and energy management solutions across Chile, Colombia, and other Latin American markets. Under the partnership, Stem will provide its PowerTrack software and controls technology, including energy management system (EMS), power plant controls (PPC), and cloud-based monitoring and analytics, while Copec Flux will lead local engineering, project delivery, and operational services. The joint offering will serve utility-scale developers and asset owners as well as commercial and industrial (C&I) customers, providing a coordinated approach to deploying, monitoring, and managing solar, storage, and hybrid energy assets.

◦On November 12, 2026, the Company announced that Voltares, an independent renewable energy developer focused on emerging markets, has selected Stem to deliver its integrated PowerTrack™ solution for Marneuli-I, a 68 MW solar facility in Marneuli, Georgia. The Marneuli-I project is the country’s first utility-scale solar facility and further expands Stem’s presence in Eastern Europe.

◦On November 3, 2026, the Company announced the successful deployment of its PowerTrack™ Platform at Border Basin Solar, a 120 MW, utility-scale solar project, owned and developed by Energix US, LLC. Border Basin is now operational and delivering clean energy into the PJM Interconnection market. Stem supported the project from conceptual design to factory preparation through commissioning and operator handoff and PowerTrack now serves as power plant control, data acquisition and monitoring backbone for Border Basin.

◦On August 12, 2026, the Company announced that Solarmarkt Group, together with engineering, procurement, and construction partner Pannonwatt Energetikai Megoldások Zrt., selected Stem's PowerTrack™ EMS as the integrated energy management, power plant control (PPC), and SCADA platform for the hybridization of two operating utility-scale solar plants in Hungary. The projects are expected to add a 40 MW / 80 MWh battery energy storage system to each of Solarmarkt Group's existing 60 MWp solar facilities. Stem previously supplied the PPC capabilities for the solar assets in 2025 and 2026 using a hybrid-ready PowerTrack architecture. Under the new agreement, the same software and controls foundation are expected to expand across the battery systems at each site, helping to enable solar generation, storage, grid interconnection, and market dispatch to operate through a single integrated solution.

◦On July 1, 2026, the Company announced that its PowerTrack EMS won The smarter E AWARD 2026 in the Smart Integrated Energy category. Organized by Solar Promotion International GmbH and Freiburg Management and Marketing International GmbH, The smarter E AWARD is presented across five industry categories, honoring pioneers of the energy industry driving major innovations across energy storage and smart integrated energy. The Smart Integrated Energy category specifically highlights technologies advancing the integration, management, and performance of clean energy systems. The award was announced during The smarter E Europe in Munich, Europe's largest alliance of exhibitions for the energy industry, bringing together Intersolar Europe, ees Europe, Power2Drive Europe, and EM-Power Europe.

Use of Non-GAAP Financial Measures
In addition to financial results determined in accordance with U.S. generally accepted accounting principles (“GAAP”), this earnings press release contains the following non-GAAP financial measures: adjusted EBITDA, non-GAAP gross profit and non-GAAP gross margin.

We use these non-GAAP financial measures for financial and operational decision-making and to evaluate our operating performance and prospects, develop internal budgets and financial goals, and facilitate period-to-period comparisons. Management believes that these non-GAAP financial measures provide meaningful supplemental information regarding our performance and liquidity by excluding certain expenses and expenditures that may not be indicative of our operating performance, such as stock-based compensation and other non-cash charges, as well as discrete cash charges that are infrequent in nature. We believe that both management and investors benefit from referring to these non-GAAP financial measures in assessing our performance and when planning, forecasting, and analyzing future periods. These non-GAAP financial measures also facilitate management’s internal comparisons to our historical performance and liquidity as well as comparisons to our competitors’ operating results, to the extent that competitors define these metrics in the same manner that we do. We believe these non-GAAP financial measures are useful to investors both because they (1) allow for greater transparency with respect to key metrics used by management in its financial and operational decision-making and (2) are used by investors and analysts to help them analyze the health of our business. Our calculation of these non-GAAP financial measures may differ from similarly titled non-GAAP measures, if any, reported by other companies. In addition, other companies may not publish these or similar measures. These non-GAAP financial measures should be considered in addition to, not as a substitute for, or superior to, other measures of financial performance prepared in accordance with GAAP. For reconciliation of adjusted EBITDA and non-GAAP gross profit and margin to their most comparable GAAP measures, see the section below entitled “Reconciliations of Non-GAAP Financial Measures.”
Definitions of Non-GAAP Financial Measures
We define adjusted EBITDA as net (loss) income attributable to Stem before depreciation and amortization, including amortization of internally developed software, interest expense, further adjusted to exclude stock-based compensation and other income and expense items, including change in fair value of warrant liability, impairment of assets held for sale, gain on extinguishment of debt, gain on energy storage systems sale, the recovery of receivables previously write-off, impairment of energy storage systems, and income tax provision or benefit. The expenses and other items that we exclude in our calculation of adjusted EBITDA may differ from the expenses and other items, if any, that other companies exclude when calculating adjusted EBITDA.

We define non-GAAP gross profit as gross profit excluding amortization of capitalized software & developed technology, and impairments related to decommissioning of end-of-life systems. We define non-GAAP gross margin as non-GAAP gross profit as a percentage of revenue.

Key Definitions:

Item
Definition
ARRAnnualized value from operating customer subscription contracts, including solar software, storage software & recurring managed services, and any recurring professional services contracts.
Bookings
Total value of executed customer purchase orders, as of the end of the relevant period (e.g. quarterly bookings or annual bookings). Customer purchase orders are typically executed three to six months ahead of hardware installation. The booking amount includes (1) hardware revenue, which is typically recognized at delivery of the energy storage hardware system and/or edge device to the customer, and (2) services revenue, which represents total nominal software and services contract value recognized ratably over the contract period.
Battery Hardware Resale RevenueSales of energy storage systems.
CARRAnnualized value from Stem customer subscription contracts with executed purchase orders signed in the period for systems that are not yet operating, and all operating Stem customer subscription contracts, including solar software, storage software & recurring managed services, and some recurring professional services contracts.
Contracted Backlog
Total value of hardware and non-recurring services bookings with executed purchase orders in dollars, as reflected on a specific date. Backlog increases as new purchase orders are executed (bookings) and decreases as hardware is delivered and recognized as revenue and as services are provided.
Edge HardwareSales of edge device hardware to aid in the collection of site data and the real-time operation and control of a site.
Operating Cash FlowNet cash provided by (used in) operating activities. Does not represent the change in balance sheet cash which will be further impacted by investing and financing activities.
Project and Professional Services Revenue
Full lifecycle energy services including development and engineering, procurement and integration, performance and operations support, and revenue tied to Development Company investments.
Solar Operating AUM
Total GW of solar systems in operation.
PowerTrack Software RevenueRecurring SaaS revenue from PowerTrack software.
Storage Operating AUM
Total GWh of energy storage systems in operation.
Managed Services RevenueIncludes (1) recurring revenue related to the operation and optimization of energy storage and hybrid portfolios managed by Stem and (2) Host Customer recurring and merchant revenues.
